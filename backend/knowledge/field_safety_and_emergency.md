# Construction Site Field Safety & Emergency Mechanical Protocols

### 1. High-Pressure Fluid Injection Injury Warning
- **Extreme Danger**: Hydraulic fluid escaping under 150 bar to 350 bar (2,100 to 5,000 psi) can penetrate heavy leather work gloves and human skin like a hypodermic needle.
- **Rules**:
  - NEVER use bare hands, gloved fingers, or skin to inspect a suspected hydraulic hose leak.
  - ALWAYS use a piece of cardboard or wood held at arm's length to check for pinhole leaks.
  - If fluid injection occurs, it is a surgical emergency. The injected chemical causes rapid compartment syndrome, gangrene, and amputation unless debrided immediately within 4-6 hours.

---

### 2. Machine Lockout / Tagout (LOTO) Sequence
Before performing any mechanical, hydraulic, or electrical service:
1. Park machine on solid, level ground away from digging faces or travel paths.
2. Lower all attachments (boom, arm, bucket, blade) firmly to the ground so no hydraulic load is suspended.
3. Idle engine for 3-5 minutes to allow turbocharger bearings to cool.
4. Turn ignition key OFF and remove key from cab.
5. Release stored hydraulic pilot pressure:
   - Turn key to ON position (engine OFF).
   - Move pilot safety control lever (gate valve) to UNLOCKED position.
   - Actuate all pilot joysticks and travel levers in all directions 10-15 times to bleed pilot accumulator pressure.
   - Slowly loosen hydraulic tank air breather to vent positive pressure from reservoir.
6. Switch master battery disconnect switch to OFF and lock with personal safety padlock.
7. Attach "DO NOT OPERATE - MAINTENANCE IN PROGRESS" warning tag to steering wheel or cab door handle.

---

### 3. Engine Runaway Emergency Shutdown
- If diesel engine begins to overspeed uncontrollably (feeding on ingested crankcase oil or vapor):
  - Do NOT attempt to turn off ignition switch (a diesel feeding on engine oil cannot be shut down by fuel shutoff).
  - Immediately cut off air intake supply: Block air intake duct with a flat metal board or discharge a CO2 fire extinguisher directly into the air cleaner inlet to starve the cylinders of oxygen.
  - Never place bare hands or soft items (towels/rags) over the intake as they can be ingested into turbocharger blades.

---

### 4. Suspended Load & Boom Hose Rupture Safety (Boom Holding Check Valve)
- **Hose Burst Hazard Under Elevated Load**: When an excavator boom is raised with a loaded bucket, hydraulic fluid in the boom cylinder base chamber is pressurized under extreme static load (typically 200 to 350 bar). If a flexible hydraulic hose ruptures or shears:
  - **Instant Drop Prevention**: The boom is prevented from dropping instantly by a **Boom Anti-Drift / Hose Rupture Holding Check Valve** (often termed Boom Holding Valve or Load Lowering Safety Valve).
  - **Mounting Location**: This safety valve is **flange-mounted directly to the cylinder base port** (not remotely on the main control valve). Mounting directly to the cylinder base port ensures that if any flexible rubber hose or hydraulic line between the main control valve and the cylinder bursts, hydraulic fluid remains trapped inside the cylinder barrel, mechanically preventing an uncontrolled gravity drop.
  - **Operating Principle**: The valve functions as a pilot-operated check valve. Free flow is permitted when pumping oil into the cylinder to raise the boom. In order to lower the boom, pilot control pressure from the operator joystick must actively unseat the check valve spool. If a supply hose bursts, pilot pressure drops to zero or load back-pressure immediately seats the check valve, locking the cylinder in position.
  - **Inspection & Service**:
    - Regularly inspect the holding valve body and mounting flange bolts for oil weeping or stress cracks.
    - If boom cylinder downward drift exceeds 50 mm in 15 minutes under rated load, inspect the boom holding valve check spool and replace worn internal check seals (e.g. P/N B210780000045).
